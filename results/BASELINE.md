WNS is Worst Negative Slack, TNS is Total Negative Slack. Positive WNS means timing
is met with slack to spare; negative WNS means the design misses timing at its
shipped clock.

## The finding that shapes the evaluation protocol (Step 2)

The corpus splits cleanly in two at the shipped clock period:

- **Timing is met (positive WNS, zero TNS):** ibex, jpeg, mock-cpu. Here area is a
  clean objective: a recipe that shrinks area at non-negative slack is unambiguously
  better.
- **Timing is missed (negative WNS):** gcd, uart, aes, ethmac, mock-alu, riscv32i.
  Here raw area is contaminated. When a design misses timing, the flow spends area
  (upsizing and buffering) chasing slack, so "smaller area" can just mean "gave up
  on timing sooner." Comparing raw area across recipes here is not apples-to-apples.

This is exactly why the headline must be stated **at matched delay**, not on raw
area. A defensible comparison needs one of:

1. **Iso-timing area:** compare area only among recipes that meet timing (WNS >= 0),
   so area is measured at a common timing bar. Cleanest, but on the timing-failing
   designs no recipe may reach WNS >= 0 at the shipped clock.
2. **Relax the clock per design** so resyn2 lands near WNS = 0, then compare recipes
   at that period. Puts every design in the clean iso-timing regime, at the cost of
   re-tuning the constraint per design.
3. **A combined figure of merit** (area plus a slack penalty) that scores the
   area/timing trade-off jointly. Simple, but the penalty weight is arbitrary and
   invites the "tune the metric to win" criticism.

Decision pending before the Step 3 label sweep. The label sweep should log all raw
metrics regardless, so the protocol can be chosen or changed after the fact.
